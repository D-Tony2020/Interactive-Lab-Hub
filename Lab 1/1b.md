## Part A. Plan 

Topic: “HoloSwing” – Futuristic Motion-Sensing Tennis Racket for Indoor Practice

**Background & Pain Point**

Many tennis enthusiasts want to practice their strokes at home, but real balls are impractical indoors—too noisy, potentially damaging, and disturbing to neighbors. At the same time, swinging without a ball lacks feedback and immersion.
This product addresses that pain point by combining visual (light), haptic (vibration), and audio (impact sounds) feedback, enabling realistic solo or multiplayer tennis experiences without a physical ball.

**Setting**

A living room or indoor recreational space. Two players (or one solo player) hold futuristic tennis rackets with built-in displays, LED lighting, and haptics.

**Activity**

Players start the session by pressing a button; rackets light up with a “READY” signal.

Virtual balls are served, shown on the racket’s semi-transparent display and guided by LED cues.

Players swing and rally, with each hit producing visual bursts of light, racket vibration, and sound effects, simulating real contact.

Different light colors represent swing strength.

The match ends with celebratory visuals (“WIN” / “GOOD GAME”).

**Goals**

Enable At-Home Tennis Training: Provide realistic practice without real balls, avoiding noise and damage.

Deliver Multi-Sensory Feedback: Use light, vibration, and sound to recreate the sensation of real hits.

Encourage Social Play: Allow multiplayer rallies and gamified competition in limited spaces.

## Part B. Act out the Interaction

We role-played with mock rackets (using cardboard + flashlight).

On paper: Sequences felt logical.

In acting: Timing of light/vibration cues proved essential—too slow and feedback felt fake.

New ideas emerged: add combo challenges (hit sequences) to gamify training.

## Part C. Prototype the device

We used smartphone screens taped into cardboard racket frames:


Vibration motor = phone’s haptic feedback.

Flashlight app = simulate color lighting.

Feedback on “Tinkerbelle” Tool (light simulation):

Good for rapid prototyping light cues.

Hard to sync between two players’ devices → future version could use Wi-Fi / Bluetooth.


## Part D. Wizard the device
